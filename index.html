Study scope & limits

A single-user workbench for reliability, availability, maintainability, and safety studies. Define a system, examine hazards through HAZOP, FMEA/FMECA and bow ties, calculate supported RAM and static fault-tree models, and connect requirements with verification and actions. Retain analyses, compare baselines and export a study reader. Studies save on your device, with backup, restore and revision-conflict recovery; a local app is also available. Analysis methods have explicit limits, and engineering qualification remains open.

How the atlas grew

Put each story back in its world. Explore people, places, and events across regions and time, in English and Chinese. Follow guided readings, compare histories side by side, and keep a personal notebook with bookmarks and notes.

I started this atlas while telling my daughter history stories: where did each story happen, and what was happening elsewhere? English and Chinese let us explore the wider world while staying connected to our Chinese heritage. Inspired by DeanChensj and the contributors to Jiangshan Map (江山时序), the atlas includes attributed adaptations of their work and is now independently maintained. Historical views and modern reference geography are labeled separately; coverage remains selective.

SE Tailoring Model

Research with Cleo Wong

Assess project drivers, inspect process recommendations, record decisions and prepare a report. Developed with Cleo Wong; research evaluation is in progress.

The tailoring approach

A research framework, developed with Cleo Wong, for right-sizing systems engineering rigor to project drivers, process interdependencies, culture and delivery risk. Work through an assessment, inspect process recommendations, record decisions and prepare a report. The updated assessment and report keep completion state, review actions and method boundaries visible; evaluation remains in progress.

One process, different projects.

Risk, complexity, and delivery conditions vary. A process checklist alone cannot explain where to invest more effort or where a lighter approach makes sense.

Make the reasoning inspectable.

The framework connects project drivers with process rigor and interdependencies. I developed the tailoring approach with Cleo Wong and built a browser tool to explore it.

A tool you can question.

Explore the assessment, inspect process recommendations, and review the guidance behind them. It is a research tool; evaluation of the method remains in progress.

Methods & principles

Systems integration and readiness

  • Interface ownership that survives organizational boundaries
  • Acceptance paths connected to evidence and readiness
  • Technical forums that move decisions instead of recycling status

MBSE and digital engineering adoption

  • Model-based work tied to real review questions
  • Traceability that exposes assumptions and gaps
  • Tooling choices shaped by team behavior and governance

Evidence, assurance, and decision memory

  • Requirements-to-verification paths people can follow
  • Assurance reviews with sharper entry and exit criteria
  • Decision records that support handover and future learning

Practice leadership and capability building

  • Right-sized systems engineering adoption patterns
  • Workshop formats that change how people decide
  • Practice assets that can outlive a single project
  1. 01

    Frame

    Define the system boundary, stakeholder landscape, operating context, success criteria, and decisions the work must support.

  2. 02

    Tailor

    Fit the systems engineering rigor to risk, novelty, safety, regulatory burden, delivery pressure, and team maturity.

  3. 03

    Connect

    Link requirements, interfaces, models, hazards, V&V, assurance evidence, and readiness claims into one inspectable thread.

  4. 04

    Transfer

    Turn delivery lessons into reusable playbooks, templates, field notes, workshop formats, diagrams, tools, and training aids.

  • Start with the decision, not the artifact.

    A model, register, workshop, or report earns its place only if it improves a decision, exposes a tradeoff, or changes what the team does next.

  • Fit the rigor to the risk.

    Over-prescribing systems engineering burns trust; under-scoping it hides risk. Tailoring has to make both sides of that trade visible.

  • Make interfaces owned, not just listed.

    Interface work matters when owners, assumptions, timing, evidence, and acceptance responsibilities are clear across organizations.

  • Keep evidence close to readiness.

    Requirements, hazards, tests, concessions, operations constraints, and residual risk should converge into a defensible readiness position.

Working paper

Applying Adaptability Practices to Transit Concepts of Operations

How should a transit concept of operations respond when service needs, operating assumptions, or system configurations change? This paper draws on published cases and established adaptability practices to connect operating intent with justified revisions to requirements, design, and operating arrangements over long development and service lives.

Abstract and outline in development

Designing for the Next Upgrade: Applying Adaptability to Rail and Transit Procurement

Examining how today's requirements and architecture choices shape tomorrow's upgrades. Published procurement and migration cases inform review questions and requirement-drafting patterns for interfaces, configuration management, migration, and support, including the engineering effort, service disruption, and cost of preserving future options.

Abstract and outline in development

Beyond System Completion: Planning for Operable and Adaptable Transit Facilities

Contract completion and subsystem tests leave a further question: can the whole facility deliver its intended service with the people, procedures, and maintenance support available? This paper uses mission analysis, human systems integration, validation, and adaptability practices to connect service outcomes with requirements, responsibilities, and evidence before handover and during later change.

Standards development in progress

INCOSE–APTA Systems Lifecycle Engineering (SLE)

Helping advance the proposed SLE standard through a transit lifecycle framework, reusable practice modules, and guidance for applying them to different projects. Current preparation focuses on lifecycle responsibilities, interfaces, tailoring, and evidence, with scoped volunteer authoring and lead/deputy continuity. The working material remains a development candidate, not an approved standard.
